A guide is only as good as the way it was made, so here is the whole process.

## From a web page to a fact

1. An AI assistant searches the web for facts on one topic, for example what to feed worms.
2. For each fact it must give the web address and copy a short quote from that page, word for word.
3. A program opens the page itself and looks for the quote. If the quote is not there, the fact is thrown away. The program also checks that every number and every name in the fact appears in the quote.
4. The program grades the website from its address. Government, university and journal sites rank highest. Copies of other people's papers on file-sharing sites are never used.

## From facts to a guide

5. A person chooses which checked facts may be published.
6. The assistant arranges only those facts into a guide. A program checks every sentence: it must cite a fact by number, and it may not add a number, a name or a strong word such as "always" that its sources do not have.
7. A person reads the guide before it goes live.

## What we cannot check

- Whether the source itself is right. We show the source so you can judge it.
- Whether a reworded sentence keeps the exact meaning of its source. This is why a person reads every guide, and why each sentence shows the original words.
- Conditions. A number from one trial may not fit your site. Read the source before you rely on it.

If you find a mistake, tell us and we will fix it.
