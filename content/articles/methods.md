Vermicomposting systems differ in output, space, labour and purpose. [1](#s1) [2](#s2) [3](#s3) [4](#s4)

## Heaps and pits

In an ICRISAT field comparison, heap vermicomposting produced 51 kg of vermicompost. [1](#s1) The pit method produced 40 kg in the same comparison. [1](#s1)

## Beds and bins

Contained beds and bins require considerable surface area. [2](#s2) Stacking bins save floor space but require lifting containers full of wet material. [2](#s2)

## Wedge systems

The wedge system modifies a windrow so worms migrate away from finished vermicompost. [3](#s3) This reduces harvesting labour compared with systems that require worm separation. [3](#s3)

## Continuous flow

Continuous-flow reactors generally suit vermicompost production rather than worm production. [4](#s4) Their efficient vermicompost production offsets their high capital cost. [4](#s4)
